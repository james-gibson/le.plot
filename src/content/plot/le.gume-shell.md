# le.gume-shell

Most of these children answer "what is this". This one answers "what is running, and what happens
if I stop it".

`le.gume-shell` keeps all seven sections — the only child that does — and changes the question
instead. Its front door is the lab: an alarm, a dashboard, a proxy and a tick, four things on a
shelf, each with a cost. It adds a `lab-operator` seed to the shelf: someone who has found the lab
and needs to know what is running before touching it.

## What it changed

- **The audience ladder.** It keeps the starter's shelf and adds one rung-3 seed for the operator.
  The rungs are the same pattern; the people are not.
- **Five isotopes.** `cornichon`, `silverskin`, `dill`, `gherkin`, `piccalilli` — the deepest
  isotope shelf of any child, and the one place where the pickle name is doing the most work.
- **The copy.** Every section is rewritten around the machine rather than around the document.

## What it proves

That a seed's *shape* survives its subject. Seven sections, five isotopes, one installer's worth of
scripts, and none of it about documentation. The content model is the thing being inherited; the
topic is free.

## What it costs

- **A front door for the wrong reader.** A rung-3 entrance in front of a rung-0 visitor ends the
  visit. The site knows this and says so; the alternative was a second home page.
- **One commit.** This child has the shortest history in the plot — one seed commit — so there is
  almost no evidence yet about what it costs to keep in step. That is a fact about the record, not
  a fault in the site.
- **Five isotopes to keep honest.** Each is a page and a registry entry; the parent's guard checks
  that a page declares its slots and presumptions, and nothing checks that the procedure still
  works. Naming five is a promise to re-read five.
